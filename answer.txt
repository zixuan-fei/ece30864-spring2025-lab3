answer A : 
pros: 
Flexibility and Adaptability: Agile allows for changes in project requirements even after the initial planning has been completed, making it ideal for projects where user needs evolve or market conditions can change rapidly.
Customer Satisfaction: With regular iterations and frequent feedback, customers are involved throughout the development process, leading to a product that more closely meets their needs and expectations.
Improved Quality: Regular reviews, testing during each iteration, and continuous integration help identify and fix defects early, improving the overall quality of the software.

cons: 
Less Predictability: Due to its flexible nature, it can be harder to predict the final delivery date and total costs of a project as scope and requirements may evolve.
Scope Creep: Without careful management, the scope of the project can expand beyond initial estimates due to the ongoing incorporation of new ideas and features.
Requires More Time and Commitment: Agile demands significant time and involvement from stakeholders and team members, which can be challenging if participants have other responsibilities

answer B :

The Scrum Master facilitates the Scrum process, helps remove impediments, and supports the development team in delivering the product efficiently. They ensure adherence to Agile practices and serve as a coach to the team, fostering an environment conducive to effective collaboration.

The Product Owner is responsible for defining the product vision, managing the product backlog, and ensuring that the team delivers value to the business. They prioritize backlog items based on business value and are the primary point of contact for stakeholders regarding the product's requirements and progress.

1. Sprint Planning
Objective Setting: Determine what to achieve in the sprint.
Task Breakdown: Discuss how to accomplish these objectives.
2. Daily Scrum (Daily Stand-Up)
Progress Updates: Team members report what they've done and will do.
Blockers: Identify any impediments affecting their tasks.
3. Sprint Review
Demonstration: Present completed work to stakeholders.
Feedback Collection: Discuss what went well and what needs improvement.

answer D:
1.Epic
Definition: A large body of work that contains related tasks, stories, and bugs.
Usage: Helps in grouping smaller, related items that contribute to a significant feature or objective over multiple sprints.
2. Story (User Story)
Definition: A smaller, actionable component of work that delivers value to the user, typically focusing on a specific feature or functionality.
Usage: Specifies functionality from an end-user perspective and includes acceptance criteria to define what needs to be done.
3. Bug
Definition: Represents a problem causing a defect or error in the system that needs to be fixed.
Usage: Used to track defects that need resolution to ensure the product works as intended.
4. Task
Definition: A small, specific piece of work required to complete a story or an epic.
Usage: Used to break down stories into manageable units of work that can be assigned and tracked individually.
